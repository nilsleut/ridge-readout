The untrained-network advantage at V1 is not geometric: separating gain from geometry in model-brain RSA

ABSTRACT (1822 characters including spaces; generated from paper/drafts/abstract.tex with
macro values from paper/numbers_map.csv; citations removed):
Untrained convolutional networks have been reported to rival trained networks as models of visual cortex. In previous work we reported such an advantage at V1 in the THINGS fMRI data, together with a dependence on evaluation resolution. Here we test this result. In a pre-specified analysis, a fitted ridge readout did not reproduce it: the untrained network scored below a backpropagation-trained network at every pooling grid (untrained minus trained median r of -0.043 at g = 4, 0 of 5 seeds positive). In exploratory analyses with criteria fixed before each step, the representational similarity analysis (RSA) advantage (+0.030 [0.018, 0.042]) and its resolution dependence disappeared under correlation distance when model units were standardised over the stimulus set (-0.011 [-0.017, -0.005]). Raw correlation-distance RSA tracked how a model's per-stimulus gain axis covaried with that of the voxels (Spearman 0.97 across our networks, 0.79 across six ImageNet architectures). Across 36 combinations of unit normalisation, distance measure and voxel normalisation, the sign of the V1 ranking was not identifiable, whereas the LOC ranking favoured trained networks wherever it was resolved. Decomposing Euclidean distances into a norm and an angular component, trained networks were ahead on the angular (geometry) component at V1 in our network and in 5 of 6 ImageNet models, and at LOC in all seven. A two-feature luminance and contrast model scored above every network under the ridge readout at V1 (point estimates for three subjects). Intervals are stimulus-bootstrap intervals for three subjects, and 5 pre-stated predictions were not met. We recommend centring units over the evaluated stimuli on both model and brain side, reporting gain and geometry components separately, and including trivial baselines.
