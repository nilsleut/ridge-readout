Untrained convolutional networks have been reported to rival or beat trained networks as models of
primary visual cortex (V1)~\citep{truzzi2020, kazemian2025}, and we had reported such an advantage
in THINGS fMRI~\citep{evalres}. Under a pre-specified ridge readout, the trained network was ahead at
every pooling grid in THINGS and in NSD (\NsdRidgeVoneGFourNeg{} of 8 subjects), also when voxel
responses were centred within runs. Representational similarity analysis (RSA) in THINGS depended on
analysis choices (\MultiDistinct{} analyses: intervals above 0 in \MultiDistinctPos, below 0 in
\MultiDistinctNeg, including 0 in \MultiDistinctIncl); in NSD with its standard normalisation, none
of \MvNsessionDistinct{} analyses favoured the untrained network. The untrained advantage appears
when a response profile common to all trials is left in the voxel patterns and model units are not
centred, under correlation distance: removing the session-mean profile in THINGS reduced it from
$\DecThreeVoneVZero$ to $\DecThreeVoneVOne$; with raw betas it reached $\CvTsevTrawVoneRawCorr$ in
THINGS, appeared in the THINGS test images, and appeared in NSD ($\CvNsdNrawVoneRawCorrKOne$,
\CvNsdNrawVoneRawCorrKOnePos{} of 8 subjects, a pre-specified prediction), where it was confined to
this one of \MvNrawDistinct{} analyses. With the profile left in, both representational
dissimilarity matrices are dominated by per-image response amplitude, which in V1 tracks contrast
energy, and the untrained network's matrix is more amplitude-dominated; the amplitude itself is
matched equally by both networks; removing the amplitude structure from both matrices removes the
advantage in NSD ($\PbNsdNrawVoneKOneNone$ to $\PbNsdNrawVoneKOneSum$, \PbNsdNrawVoneKOneSumLe{} of 8
subjects). A luminance-and-contrast baseline outperformed every network under ridge in both datasets
(point estimates); the networks reached \CeilVoneFracMin--\CeilVoneFracMax\% of the NSD noise-ceiling
lower bound. \FailedPredictions{} of \TotalPredictions{} pre-specified predictions were not met. We
recommend centring or normalising voxel responses within session, reporting the sensitivity of RSA
rankings to this choice and to model-unit centring, and replicating on a second dataset.
