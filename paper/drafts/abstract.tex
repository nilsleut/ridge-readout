Untrained convolutional networks have been reported to match early visual cortex as
well as or better than trained networks in representational similarity analysis
(RSA)~\citep{TODO-untrained}. In previous work we reported such an advantage at V1 in
the THINGS fMRI data, together with a dependence on evaluation resolution
\citep{evalres}. Here we test this result. In a pre-specified analysis, a fitted ridge
readout reversed it: the untrained network scored below a backpropagation-trained
network at every pooling grid (untrained minus trained median $r$ of
$\PreregRandBpGFour$ at $g = 4$, 0 of 5 seeds positive). In exploratory analyses with
criteria fixed before each step, the RSA advantage ($+\VoneGapRawTwoTwoFour$) and its
resolution dependence disappeared when model units were standardised over the stimulus
set ($\VoneGapZTwoTwoFour$). Raw correlation-distance RSA tracked how a model's
per-stimulus gain axis covaried with that of the voxels (Spearman \GOneSpearman{} across
our networks, \GenOneSpearman{} across six ImageNet architectures). Across 36
combinations of unit normalisation, distance measure and voxel normalisation, the sign
of the V1 ranking was not identifiable, whereas the LOC ranking favoured trained
networks wherever it was resolved. Decomposing Euclidean distances into a norm and an
angular component, trained networks were ahead on the angular (geometry) component at
V1 in our network and in \SepThreeModels{} of 6 ImageNet models, and at LOC in all
seven. A two-feature luminance and contrast model exceeded every network under the
ridge readout at V1. We recommend centring units over the evaluated stimuli on both
model and brain side, reporting gain and geometry components separately, and including
trivial baselines.
