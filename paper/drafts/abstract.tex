Untrained convolutional networks have been reported to rival trained networks as models of
visual cortex~\citep{truzzi2020, kazemian2025}, and we had reported an advantage of an
untrained network over a backpropagation-trained one at V1 in the THINGS fMRI data,
together with a dependence on evaluation resolution~\citep{evalres}. Under a pre-specified
ridge readout, the untrained network scored below the trained one at every pooling grid
(median $r$ difference $\PreregRandBpGFour$ at $g = 4$, \PreregRandBpGFourPos{} of 5 seeds
positive), and also in MSE skill and per-fold $r$. In exploratory analyses, across 36 combinations of unit
normalisation, distance measure and voxel normalisation, the sign of the V1 contrast
depended on the analysis (\MultiPosCiCells{} cells with intervals above 0,
\MultiNegCiCells{} below 0), and in our networks the resolution dependence of the original convention appeared in only one cell. Raw correlation-distance representational similarity analysis (RSA) covaried with the alignment of model
and voxel patterns with the unit-mean profile (Spearman \GOneSpearman{} across our networks,
\GenOneSpearman{} across six ImageNet architectures); the advantage was absent from the
component orthogonal to the mean profile, and the parallel component alone did not resolve
it. On the unweighted angular component of centred patterns, trained networks were ahead at
V1 (our network and \SepThreeModels{} of 6 ImageNet models) and at LOC (all seven); the
norm-weighted angular term was not resolved. A luminance-and-contrast bank scored above
every network under ridge (point estimates, three subjects), and adding network features
to it did not improve prediction. Intervals are stimulus-bootstrap intervals for three
subjects, and \FailedPredictions{} pre-stated predictions were not met. We recommend
centring units over the evaluated stimuli on both model and brain side, reporting
components separately together with a small multiverse, and including simple baselines.
