Untrained convolutional networks have been reported to rival trained networks as
models of visual cortex~\citep{truzzi2020, kazemian2025}. In previous work we reported such an advantage at V1 in
the THINGS fMRI data, together with a dependence on evaluation resolution
\citep{evalres}. Here we test this result. In a pre-specified analysis, a fitted ridge
readout did not reproduce it: the untrained network scored below a backpropagation-trained
network at every pooling grid (untrained minus trained median $r$ of
$\PreregRandBpGFour$ at $g = 4$, \PreregRandBpGFourPos{} of 5 seeds positive). In exploratory analyses with
criteria fixed before each step, the representational similarity analysis (RSA)
advantage ($+\VoneGapRawTwoTwoFour$ [$\VoneGapRawTwoTwoFourLo$, $\VoneGapRawTwoTwoFourHi$]) and its
resolution dependence disappeared under correlation distance when model units were standardised over the stimulus set ($\VoneGapZTwoTwoFour$ [$\VoneGapZTwoTwoFourLo$, $\VoneGapZTwoTwoFourHi$]). Raw correlation-distance RSA tracked how a model's mean-profile alignment covaried with that of the voxels (Spearman \GOneSpearman{} across
our networks, \GenOneSpearman{} across six ImageNet architectures). Across 36
combinations of unit normalisation, distance measure and voxel normalisation, the sign of the V1 ranking depended on the analysis, whereas the LOC ranking favoured trained
networks wherever it was resolved. Decomposing Euclidean distances into a norm and an
angular component, trained networks were ahead on the angular component at
V1 in our network and in \SepThreeModels{} of 6 ImageNet models, and at LOC in all
seven. A luminance-and-contrast bank scored above every network under the ridge readout at V1 (point estimates for three subjects). Intervals are stimulus-bootstrap intervals for three subjects, and \FailedPredictions{} pre-stated predictions were not met. We recommend centring units over the evaluated stimuli on both
model and brain side, reporting norm and angular components separately, and including
trivial baselines.
