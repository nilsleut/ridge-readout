Whether untrained networks beat trained ones at V1 depends on a normalisation convention. In two fMRI data
sets, a pre-specified ridge readout placed the trained network ahead, and correlation-distance RSA favoured
the untrained network only when a voxel response profile common to all trials was left in the patterns and
the model units were not centred. A prediction committed before the NSD data were reloaded confirmed that
this convention creates the advantage in a second data set. Under that convention both RDMs are dominated by
per-image response amplitude, the untrained network's more so, and removing this amplitude structure from
both RDMs removed the advantage in NSD. Normalising voxel responses within session, reporting the
sensitivity of rankings to this choice and to model-unit centring, and replicating on a second data set make
such comparisons interpretable.
