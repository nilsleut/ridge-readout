\section{Methods}
\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We used the THINGS-fMRI data set \cite{TODO-thingsfmri} of 3 subjects. Single-trial
betas were taken from the ROI masks of the voxel metadata (V1; LOC as the union of lLOC
and rLOC) and z-scored per voxel across all 9{,}840 trials of the subject before stimulus
selection. The 720 evaluation stimuli are one exemplar each of 720 distinct concepts,
in the same order for all subjects; all are single-presentation \texttt{train} trials
from one session per subject.

Voxel reliability was measured on the 100 \texttt{test} images, shown 12 times each,
identical across subjects and disjoint from the 720. Per voxel, the Pearson
correlation between the means of two halves of 6 repetitions was averaged over all 462
balanced splits and Spearman--Brown corrected, $r_{\mathrm{SB}} = 2r/(1+r)$. A voxel is
reliable if $r_{\mathrm{SB}} > 0.2$, a threshold fixed in advance for all conditions;
measured on other images, selection cannot leak into fits on the 720. V1 contained \NReliableSubOne,
\NReliableSubTwo{} and \NReliableSubThree{} reliable voxels in sub-01, sub-02 and
sub-03. Ridge scores use reliable V1 voxels; RSA uses all voxels of the ROI.

RDMs were compared on cross-run pairs only: pairs of distinct stimuli shown in different
runs in every subject, 210{,}205 of the 258{,}840 pairs of the full set. This follows
the convention of \cite{TODO-evalres}.
% Rationale for the cross-run restriction: not stated in the sources (see paper/METHODS_LITERALS.md).

\subsection{Models}

\paragraph{CIFAR-trained CNNs.} We evaluated the 30 checkpoints of \cite{TODO-evalres}:
6 conditions $\times$ 5 seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1337) of one convolutional
architecture, trained on CIFAR \cite{TODO-cifar} at 32\,px, the training resolution. The
conditions are Backprop, Feedback Alignment (FA), Predictive Coding (PC), STDP
\cite{TODO-learningrules} and two untrained networks: untrained (uncalibrated BN
statistics), with BatchNorm running statistics left at initialisation, and untrained
(BN-calibrated), with running statistics set by 50 forward-only passes of CIFAR batches
at 32\,px. ``Untrained'' alone means the uncalibrated variant, as in the published
headline.

The V1 layer is Conv1, the output of the whole first block (convolution, BatchNorm, ReLU,
$2 \times 2$ max pooling; 32 channels, map side half the input size); LOC is mapped to
Conv3. Features were extracted in eval mode with the original code, in two
input arms. NATIVE: the image is resized and centre-cropped
to $p \in \{32, 64, 96, 128, 160, 224\}$\,px. UPSAMPLED: the image is resized and
cropped to 32\,px and then resized to $p$, so its content is limited to 32\,px; we used
it at $p = 224$. Both arms apply the CIFAR channel normalisation.

\paragraph{ImageNet models.} Six torchvision models \cite{TODO-torchvision}: alexnet,
vgg11\_bn, resnet18, resnet50, convnext\_tiny and vit\_b\_16, in eval mode. Trained
models use the IMAGENET1K\_V1 weights. Untrained models were built without weights after
\texttt{torch.manual\_seed($s$)}, $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, with BatchNorm running statistics
at their defaults (mean 0, variance 1); convnext\_tiny and vit\_b\_16 use LayerNorm. RGB images
were resized (shorter side) and centre-cropped to $R \in \{112, 224, 448\}$ (training
resolution 224) and ImageNet-normalised; vit\_b\_16 accepts only 224. Layers (V1 / LOC),
fixed before the run: alexnet
\texttt{features[1]} / \texttt{features[12]}; vgg11\_bn \texttt{features[2]} / last max
pooling of \texttt{features}; resnet18 and resnet50 \texttt{maxpool} / \texttt{layer4};
convnext\_tiny \texttt{features[0]} / \texttt{features[7]}; vit\_b\_16
\texttt{encoder.layers[0]} / \texttt{encoder.layers[-1]}. Outputs were averaged over
space, or over tokens without the class token.

\subsection{RSA convention}

Unless stated otherwise, RSA follows \cite{TODO-evalres}. The model RDM is the
correlation distance ($1 -$ Pearson $r$ across units) between the globally average-pooled
(GAP) features of two stimuli; the fMRI RDM is the correlation distance between their
beta patterns over all ROI voxels. RSA is the Spearman correlation between the two RDMs
over the cross-run pairs, per subject, then averaged. A
contrast is the mean over seeds of the per-seed difference between two conditions; for
ImageNet models it is untrained (mean over the 3 initialisation seeds) minus trained.

Uncertainty is over stimuli: the 1000 bootstrap resamples of \cite{TODO-evalres} (720
stimuli drawn with replacement, seed 20261002). Each draw recomputes the cross-run pair
set on the resampled stimuli, dropping pairs of a stimulus with itself, and every
difference, slope and ratio is formed within the draw (paired). Intervals are 95\%
percentile intervals. They describe stimulus variation in 3 subjects, not the
population.

\subsection{Analysis multiverse}

Three factors were crossed. Feature normalisation $F$, per unit over the 720 stimuli:
raw; center (mean subtracted); scale (divided by the SD, not centred); z (both). Units
with SD below $10^{-8}$ are set to 0. Distance $D$: correlation, cosine or euclidean,
applied to features and betas alike. fMRI normalisation $B$: paper (betas
as in Section~\ref{sec:data}), center720 (centred per voxel over the 720 stimuli) and
z720 (z-scored per voxel over the 720). The cell (raw, correlation, paper) is the
convention of \cite{TODO-evalres}. All $4 \times 3 \times 3 = 36$ cells were computed for
the V1 contrast (Conv1, untrained $-$ Backprop) and the LOC contrast (Conv3, Backprop
$-$ untrained). The resolution slope is
$S = \mathrm{Gap}(\text{NATIVE } 224) - \mathrm{Gap}(\text{NATIVE } 32)$, with Gap the V1
contrast. As a check, euclidean RDMs under $F$ = raw and center must coincide. For
ImageNet models, all 36 cells were computed as point estimates and four were
bootstrapped: (raw, correlation, paper), (z, correlation, paper), (raw, euclidean,
paper) and (center, correlation, center720); their slope is
$S_{\mathrm{gen}} = \mathrm{Gap}(448) - \mathrm{Gap}(224)$.

\subsection{Ridge readout}

Encoding models used dual ridge regression \cite{TODO-ridge}, with the kernel
$K = XX^{\top}$ eigendecomposed once per split. The Conv1 maps were
pooled to a $g \times g$ grid by adaptive average pooling, $g \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$, giving
$32g^2$ features independent of resolution; $g = 1$ is GAP, and larger $g$ retains
spatial structure. Features were standardised with statistics of the training rows of
each split only.

Cross-validation is nested: 8 outer folds and 5 inner folds within each outer training
set, from a fixed partition (seed 20260818); with one exemplar per concept, folds are
concept-grouped. Per voxel and outer fold, $\alpha$ was
chosen from 29 log-spaced values between $10^{-6}$ and $10^{8}$ by the minimum mean
squared error over the inner folds, and the model was refit on the outer training set.
Selection and reporting differ deliberately: $\alpha$ mainly sets the scale of the
prediction, so selecting on the scale-invariant Pearson $r$ is degenerate; reported $r$
therefore lies slightly below its attainable maximum, equally for every condition.
Out-of-fold predictions were pooled over the outer folds and scored by one Pearson $r$
per voxel over all 720 stimuli. The score is the median $r$ over reliable V1 voxels,
averaged over subjects, with contrasts formed within seed.

A low-edge selection at a reliable voxel is logged, not fatal (amendment of 2026-10-03).
With diagnostic values $10^{-7}$, $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-9}$
added, it is a valid plateau if the inner-fold MSE improves by less than a relative
$10^{-3}$ on its value at $10^{-6}$; otherwise the cell is excluded. The gate never
changes a score.

The preregistered analysis used the 720 single-trial betas at NATIVE 224\,px, all
checkpoints and subjects, and all four $g$; the $g = 2$ features were extracted on a CPU,
subject to a downstream equivalence gate (amendment of 2026-10-13). An exploratory fit
at $g = 1$ covered all NATIVE resolutions and UPSAMPLED 224.

Two controls used the identical readout. The control band consists of low-level banks
pooled to the same grid: pixels; Rec.\,709 luminance with local RMS contrast
($9 \times 9$ window); absolute responses of a Gabor bank (4 orientations $\times$
3 spatial frequencies); and, as negative test, Backprop seed-0 features with permuted
stimulus assignment. The spatial-correspondence control permutes the $g \times g$ cells
differently for every image (seed 20260820), keeping dimensionality and per-channel
distributions; it was run for the seed-0 untrained and Backprop checkpoints at
$g \in \{2, 4, 8\}$.

\subsection{Gain axis and decomposition}

For a stimulus $\times$ unit matrix $X$, let $\mu$ be the unit means over the 720
stimuli, $\mu_c = \mu - \overline{\mu}$, and $\tilde{x}_i$ row $i$ centred across units.
The gain of stimulus $i$ is the cosine between its response profile and the mean profile,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{gain}_i = \frac{\langle \tilde{x}_i, \mu_c \rangle}{\lVert \tilde{x}_i \rVert \, \lVert \mu_c \rVert}.
\end{equation}
Model gain was computed on raw features, voxel gain on all ROI voxels under $B$ = paper.
$c_{\mathrm{gain}}$ is the Pearson correlation over the 720 stimuli between model gain
and V1 voxel gain, averaged over subjects. The gain RDM is
$\lvert \mathrm{gain}_i - \mathrm{gain}_j \rvert$. Partial RSA regresses the ranks of the
model and of the fMRI RDM, per subject and draw, on an intercept and the ranks of their
own gain RDM and correlates the residuals (Pearson).

The decomposition starts from $X_c$, $X$ with every unit centred over the 720 stimuli
($F$ = center; for $F$ = z, units are also scaled to SD 1). With
$n_i = \lVert X_{c,i} \rVert$ and $\cos\theta_{ij}$ the cosine between rows $i$ and $j$,
\begin{equation}
  \lVert X_{c,i} - X_{c,j} \rVert^2 = (n_i - n_j)^2 + 2\, n_i n_j \,(1 - \cos\theta_{ij}).
\end{equation}
The euclidean RDM on $X_c$ is thus an exact function of the norm RDM
$N_{ij} = \lvert n_i - n_j \rvert$ and the angle RDM $A_{ij} = 1 - \cos\theta_{ij}$. The
betas are decomposed in the same way, and the RSA of a component compares the model and
fMRI RDMs of that component. The identity was checked numerically. We call the norm
component ``gain magnitude'' and the angle component ``geometry''; both labels define the
two terms of this identity and are not biological concepts.
